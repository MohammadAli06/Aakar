**Buyer Requirement Matches Engine (Title-First Multi-Parameter Scoring), 2026-09-19:**
Enhanced `CommerceEngine.matches()` and the "Find matches" screen (`matches()` in `commerce_screen.dart`):
- **Weighted Multi-Parameter Scoring Model (0–100%):**
  - **Priority 1: Product Title / What You Need (45% weight):** Direct word hits in the artisan product's title receive double weight, followed by craft, category, material, and description. Expanded Hindi-English vocabulary mappings covering terms like `बाँस/bamboo`, `टोकरी/basket`, `मिट्टी/clay`, `कपास/सूती/cotton`, `कपड़ा/fabric`, `साड़ी/saree`, `बैग/थैला/bag`, `गमला/planter`, `दीया/diya`, `पीतल/brass`, `धातु/metal`, `लकड़ी/wood`, `सिरेमिक/ceramic`, `मग/mug`, `हथकरघा/handloom`, `कारीगरी/handcrafted`.
  - **Priority 2: Budget & Price Fit (15% weight):** Compares buyer target budget with product unit price; highlights exact price savings or percentage gap.
  - **Priority 3: Quantity, MOQ & Production Capacity (15% weight):** Verifies if requested quantity meets MOQ and checks actual committed vs. available artisan capacity.
  - **Priority 4: Lead Time & Delivery Timeline (15% weight):** Compares requested lead days against artisan lead time with explicit days-difference scoring.
  - **Priority 5: Delivery Location Match (10% weight):** Compares buyer destination with artisan workshop region (same district/state proximity boost).
- **Match Results UI:**
  - Displays a highlighted **Match Score Badge** (e.g. `95% Match` with high fit tag) on each product card.
  - **Requirement Context Banner:** Summarizes the buyer's query parameters (product title, quantity, lead days, budget, location) at the top.
  - **Explainable Fit Breakdown:** Categorizes reasons into verified capabilities (`✓ Strong title & craft match`, `✓ Within unit budget`, `✓ Nearby location match`) and clear negotiation points (`• Things to negotiate`).

**Inquiry messages raise a real notification, 2026-09-18:**
 sending a message or a voice note in an inquiry now writes a `Notification` row for the **other** participant — the sender is not notified about their own message — via `backend/app/routers/notifications.py` (`GET /api/v1/notifications/`, `POST /api/v1/notifications/read`). Each row carries a link of `inquiry/<id>?tab=chat`, and the router passes that `tab` through to `CommerceScreen`, so tapping the alert opens that conversation directly on its **Chat** tab instead of the request summary. The alerts list is no longer device-local: `CommerceRepository` loads notifications during `hydrate` (server rows carry the recipient's user id, device-local demo rows keep the role's actor id) and the bell's "mark all as read" goes to the account endpoint. Covered by two backend tests (a message notifies only the other side and the reply notifies back, marking read leaves the other side untouched; a voice note notifies too) and by `test/commerce_repository_test.dart` plus `test/inquiry_chat_tab_test.dart` (the deep link lands on the conversation, the default is still the request tab).

**Payments and settlement now persist for the order, 2026-09-18:** the buyer's payment button was engine-only, so the "Order Received!" dialog appeared while the milestone stayed unpaid and came back after a refresh. Three account-scoped endpoints were added: `PATCH /orders/{id}/pay` (buyer, per milestone, with the engine's trigger preconditions — the progress review approved for a checkpoint, production ready for the dispatch milestone, inspection accepted for final settlement), `PATCH /orders/{id}/inspection` (buyer accepts; a quantity mismatch is refused so it becomes an issue), and `PATCH /orders/{id}/complete` (buyer closes once delivered, inspected and fully settled, consuming the advertised stock like the engine does). `delivery-status` now stamps the inspection deadline on receipt, and "Mark in transit" / "Confirm delivery received" use that shared endpoint rather than engine-only `shipping`/`delivery`, so the receipt the inspection depends on survives a refresh too. The payment sheet no longer celebrates a failed write — it confirms only when the action actually succeeded. Still engine-local and **not** shared: `issue` (needs an issues store and an admin surface), `checkpoint`, `representation`, `production` (the step-6 status button, which overlaps the server-backed progress and complete steps) and the older `dispatch()` helper (overlaps the step-10 dispatch form). Covered by `backend/tests/test_orders_api.py` (the whole deliver → inspect → settle → complete chain, the artisan being refused payment, final settlement refused before inspection, and a quantity mismatch).

**Dates are picked from a calendar, 2026-09-18:** every date field in the shared craft form now opens the Material calendar instead of accepting a typed `YYYY-MM-DD` string. `CraftField` gained a `date` flag (renders a read-only field with a calendar affordance, storing `yyyy-MM-dd`) and a `withTime` flag that continues to a time picker and stores `yyyy-MM-dd HH:mm`. Applied to all seven date fields: production plan start date and expected completion, dispatch date and estimated delivery (artisan orders), the buyer's target date in requirement/inquiry forms and in the quotation form, and the on-site demo date — that last one is the **only** field that asks for a time, since the others are date-only and must not prompt for a clock time. Field labels dropped their `(YYYY-MM-DD)` hints as the picker now guarantees the format, and values keep the ISO shape the backend and existing read-only displays already expect, so no stored record or display changes. The picker clamps its initial date into the allowed range (`showDatePicker` asserts otherwise) and prefills from any existing value when reopening a form. Covered by `test/craft_date_field_test.dart` (calendar opens and stores a plain date with no time step, a date & time field continues to the clock, a required date still reports a missing value, and a plain field is still typed by hand).

**First-run coach-mark tour, 2026-09-18:** a five-stop guided tour (new dependency: `showcaseview` 5.1.0) runs once per role on the first Home visit after signup — artisan: Home, Add product, Products, Bidding, Orders; buyer: Home, Discover, Inquiries, Bidding, Orders. Each stop spotlights the real widget (the bottom-bar icon, or the Add product button) with a bilingual caption, an `n/total` counter in the bubble title and Skip/Next controls. The "seen" flag is device-local (`onboarding_seen_artisan` / `onboarding_seen_buyer` in SharedPreferences) — no backend route and no schema, so it stays off the API surface. Profile gains an **App guide** row that navigates Home and replays the same sequence **without** touching the flag, so first-run behaviour for a new account is unchanged. The replay was initially dead: the workspace keeps **one `State`** across `/workspace/:page` changes, so returning Home from Profile never re-ran `initState` (where the only tour check lived) and `_tourStarted` was never cleared — the button just went Home and nothing happened. The coach-mark scope is registered in `AakarApp.initState` rather than in `main`, so it exists wherever the app widget is built — including widget tests; registration is idempotent. The tour can never block a working screen: if the scope is unavailable the start is skipped and left unrecorded so it is offered again. The buyer bar has no Requirements destination, so requirement posting is introduced on the Home stop instead of adding a sixth stop. Covered by `test/app_tour_test.dart` (flag is per role, each role's step list matches its own bar, every stop has bilingual copy, the first run records itself, a returning user is not shown it again).

**Inquiries and quotations moved to the shared backend, 2026-09-17:** Buyer inquiries and every quotation version are account-scoped rows instead of device-local demo records, so an inquiry a buyer sends appears in the artisan's Inquiries tab on that artisan's own account. `backend/app/routers/inquiries.py` serves `POST/GET /api/v1/inquiries`, the `messages`, `capacity`, `quote`, `request-change`, `sample`, `reject`, `accept` and `voice` actions, and `POST /preview`; read-only `GET /api/v1/orders` exposes the order an accepted quotation creates. Participation and role are enforced server-side: only the artisan answers capacity, a buyer cannot accept their own quotation, a quotation cannot price below the material + labour + overhead floor or exceed confirmed capacity, milestones must total 100%, and a new capacity answer or quotation supersedes the previous open version. Accepting a quotation writes a real `Order` row both accounts read. **The inquiry screen is one shared thread** (`lib/features/commerce/presentation/inquiry_workspace.dart`): Request, Chat and Quotation tabs, an 8-second poll plus pull-to-refresh and a foreground refresh, so the other participant's reply appears without leaving the screen. A buyer can ask for specific changes to an open quotation (`request-change`), which reopens it for a revised version, and long inquiry/quotation forms carry optional step sections. ## Start the demo

From the repository root:

```powershell
flutter pub get
flutter run
```

Choose the explicit demo login. The dashboard now opens the commerce workspace. Switch Artisan / Buyer on the same device; language and business records persist. Profile offers seeded artisan selection and an explicitly confirmed local reset. Four sample product illustrations are included; camera/gallery can supply actual craft photos.

For shared mobile/admin records, install backend requirements into `backend/.venv`, then:

```powershell
cd backend
.\run_demo.ps1
```

The script sets a valid boolean DEBUG, starts `main:app` from the correct folder, uses `backend/demo.db` (SQLite), and prints separate randomly generated mobile/admin tokens. It does not need Docker or Redis for this workflow. Keep that terminal open. Existing environment tokens are retained when provided.

In mobile Profile → Connect backend workspace, enter the mobile token and base URL: `http://10.0.2.2:8000` for an Android emulator, or the computer's LAN address for a phone on the same network. Connecting loads the server workspace; it does not merge/upload the local workspace. Pull to refresh to retrieve changes from another device/admin. Failed writes remain failures; the app does not silently switch to local simulation.

Open `http://localhost:8000/api/v1/workspace/admin` on the computer and enter the **admin** token. The mobile token cannot perform admin actions. Admin verification, moderation, issue resolution, progress reviews and counts use the same database. Evidence photos require authenticated access.

## Admin console (admin-web)

The production-facing reviewer console is the React app in `admin-web/`. It replaces the earlier single-page HTML dashboard for the real account-review work; `backend/admin/index.html` remains as the legacy demo-workspace page.

Run it from `admin-web/` with `npm install` then `npm run dev` (default `http://localhost:5173`), and sign in with the `ADMIN_ACCESS_TOKEN` value. In development Vite proxies `/api` to `BACKEND_URL` (default `http://localhost:8000`), so no browser CORS setup is needed. `VITE_API_BASE_URL` overrides the API prefix for a built asset.

Implemented against the shared database:

| Screen | Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dashboard | `GET /api/v1/admin/overview`, `/admin/activity` | Counts, verification pipeline, role split, attention list |
| Verification queue | `GET /api/v1/auth/verification-admin` joined with `/admin/accounts` | Names come from the account directory; the record supplies evidence and status |
| Verification review | `GET /api/v1/auth/verification-admin/{id}/evidence/{kind}`, `PUT /api/v1/auth/verification-admin/{id}` | Approve, request correction or reject with a mandatory note; evidence is fetched per request and never cached |
| Accounts | `GET /api/v1/admin/accounts`, `GET /api/v1/admin/accounts/{id}`, `PUT /api/v1/admin/accounts/{id}/status` | Directory plus enable/disable access control |
| Products | `GET /api/v1/admin/products`, `GET /api/v1/admin/products/{id}`, `PUT /api/v1/admin/products/{id}/moderation` | Listing, cost-floor pricing, persisted moderation decision; rows are written by the mobile catalogue endpoints below |
| Requirements | `GET /api/v1/admin/requirements` | Buyer demand posted from the marketplace, with buyer, quantity, budget, lead time and destination. Read-only: a requirement is not an order, so no decision is recorded |
| Order issues | `GET`/`POST /api/v1/workspace` (admin token) | Manual review only; disabled unless `ENABLE_DEMO_WORKSPACE=true` |
| Analytics | `GET /api/v1/admin/overview` | Current-state snapshot; no time-series history is stored |
| Audit trail | `GET /api/v1/admin/activity` | `admin_audit_log` plus verification submissions |
| Platform | `GET /api/v1/admin/platform` | Read-only, non-secret backend facts |

Two tables back the new endpoints, created by the existing startup `create_all` so no migration is required for them: `product_moderation` (current decision per product) and `admin_audit_log` (who decided what). Verification review decisions now write an audit entry as well.

Boundaries kept explicit in the interface: moderation never publishes or unpublishes a listing (the artisan publishes); approving verification is invalidated by a later profile edit; the console moves no money, books no carrier and makes no external marketplace submission; and B2B monitoring, bidding/auctions, integrations and support are presented as a scope statement rather than mock screens. Legacy `verification` and `moderation` demo-workspace actions remain available to the HTML page but are no longer the primary admin path.

## Code map

- `lib/features/commerce/domain/commerce_engine.dart`: pure workflow transitions, readiness, matching and route explanations.
- `lib/features/commerce/data/commerce_repository.dart`: product actions go to the authenticated backend catalogue (see *Product catalogue*); the remaining demo flows stay local or on the opt-in shared workspace, with version conflicts, media uploads and reviewed assistant drafts.

## Back-translation validation on Listen & Verify - 2026-09-14

The Listen & Verify step now runs an advisory back-translation check. The generated Hindi description is translated back into English with the free Groq text model, then a second call to the same tier judges whether the round trip still carries the same product facts, explicitly ignoring wording and style. Scores at or above 0.75 show a "Translation checked" chip; lower scores show "Please listen carefully". Nothing is auto-regenerated: the artisan's existing voice correction remains the way to resolve a real disagreement.

This validates model-generated Hindi against its own English. The Hindi description is produced natively by the vision catalog call, not by machine translation, so only one translation direction (Hindi to English) runs per check - two free-tier calls per product, not three. Language codes are parameters, so a Marathi or Bengali listing reuses the same function unchanged.

The check never blocks or fails the artisan. An unavailable provider, a blank description pair, or a missing key returns `is_fallback: true` with `confidence_label: unavailable`; the mobile app then shows no chip rather than implying a check that did not happen. The score and label are stored on the workspace product record as `roundtrip_score` and `translation_confidence`, and an unavailable check clears any previously stored score. `method` reports which tier served the result (`free` or `openai-fallback`).

Each of the two calls tries the free tier first and the OpenAI translation model once as an explicit fallback when the free tier is unset or fails. A free-tier failure therefore costs two OpenAI calls, not five: the fallback is scoped per call, and a healthy free tier spends nothing.

Bhashini machine translation is NOT implemented. Bhashini ASR is the only Bhashini integration, and `BHASHINI_API_KEY` is still a placeholder in `backend/.env`, so ASR returns its demo fallback. ## Bidding Handoff Quotation & Capacity Synchronization - 2026-09-19

- **Issue**: In bidding sessions, after an artisan allocated lot quantities and proceeded to handoff/quotation, the buyer's app remained stuck displaying *"Waiting for the artisan's capacity response"* even when the artisan had confirmed capacity and proposed a quotation.
- **Root Causes**:
  1. Handoff initialized `'capacity_status': 'pending'` without `confirmed_quantity` or `offered_lead_days`, despite the artisan having already selected the bidder and allocated the capacity.
  2. Bidding handoff did not create a shared persistent `Inquiry` row in PostgreSQL, causing subsequent artisan capacity confirmations or quotations to be stored only in local device state without being reflected on the buyer's account or session inquiries.
  3. The inquiry workspace UI strictly checked `capacity == 'pending'` first, blocking the buyer from seeing *"Review the latest quotation and agree the terms"* even when quotes existed in `quotes`.
  4. The quotation tab gated quotation display behind `['confirmed', 'partial'].contains(capacity_status)`, displaying an error when capacity was unconfirmed.
- **Resolution**:
  1. In `backend/app/routers/bidding.py`, handoff now initializes `capacity_status='confirmed'`, sets `confirmed_quantity` and `offered_lead_days`, and persists the `Inquiry` directly to the `inquiries` table so both accounts have access to the shared record. Inquiries returned by `GET /api/v1/bidding` now sync quotes, capacity status, and order status from the DB.
  2. In `backend/app/routers/inquiries.py`, bidding inquiries auto-confirm capacity on quote proposal and relax the standard catalog MOQ check to `1` so lot allocations can be quoted freely.
  3. In `lib/features/commerce/presentation/inquiry_workspace.dart`, next-action status logic prioritizes active quotations (`hasQuotes`), transitioning the buyer to *"Review the latest quotation and agree the terms"*.
  4. In `lib/features/commerce/presentation/commerce_screen.dart`, `capacityReady` acknowledges active quotes and bidding inquiries.
  5. In `lib/features/commerce/data/commerce_repository.dart` and `commerce_engine.dart`, `importBiddingInquiry` safely merges quotations and capacity confirmation, and `case 'quote':` auto-confirms capacity on bidding inquiries.
